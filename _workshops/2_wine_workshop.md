Applies the neuroscience of flavour perception to wine, for sommeliers, winemakers, and wine educators. Drawing on current research in neuroenology, the session frames wine tasting not as passive detection but as an active construction of flavour, built from the integration of olfactory, gustatory, trigeminal, and contextual information.

Particular attention is given to how expertise reshapes this process — drawing on neuroimaging research on sommeliers - alongside practical themes such as glassware and aroma release, the neurobiology of mouthfeel, and sensory adaptation over the course of a tasting. The session closes with guided tasting exercises that connect the science directly to professional practice.

Delivered at:
Lyon, France — Institut Lyfe Research Center, for graduate wine students ("Unlocking Flavour Perception: A Coffee Sensory Neuroscience Workshop for Wine Experts" — using coffee as a controlled sensory model)
Porto, Portugal — invited industry seminar for Sogrape Wines, "The Neurophysiology of Wine Tasting"
